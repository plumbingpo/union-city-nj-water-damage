Typical Water Damage Problems in Jersey City

Ask anyone on our crew what fills the schedule in Jersey City and you will hear the same six answers. None of them is rare, and each gets worse the longer the water sits.

Mold: Most mold growth is the follow-up to an earlier leak that seemed to be taken care of. Fans and towels dry what you can see, while the wall cavity, subfloor and carpet pad stay damp underneath. Growth can begin within 24 to 48 hours, then spread quietly until you smell it or see spotting.

Hoses: Every sink, toilet and washer is fed by a flexible connector that holds full house pressure around the clock. A burst connector keeps spraying until somebody closes a valve, and that may take a long while in an empty house. The low-cost defense is to swap out aging hoses and learn where the main shutoff is.

Pumps: In homes that have one, a sump pump works unnoticed for years and then quits on the one night it is needed. What we usually see is a jammed float, a motor that has worn out or a breaker that tripped. That leaves the lowest level under a few inches of water, with flooring, wall bases and stored belongings soaked. A battery backup and a quick test before the rainy part of the year go a long way.

Toilets: Among bathroom emergencies in Jersey City, the toilet that will not stop rising tops the list. It matters where the water came from, because tank or supply water is clean and used bowl water is not. Either way it moves under tile edges and vanity cabinets, so the floor needs to be checked with a meter.

Cooling: Few people think about the small pipe that carries condensation away from an AC system. If the line plugs or the pan rusts through, water drips into the ceiling or the floor around the unit. The leak is gradual, so drywall and insulation may be soaked long before you spot a mark. Having the line flushed each year, before the cooling season, prevents most of these.

Flooding: A long, heavy rain can overwhelm street drains and send water across the yard and into the house. It reaches ground-level rooms, garages and walkout lower levels on the downhill side before anything else. We pump it out, discard materials that cannot be sanitized and keep equipment running until moisture readings are back to normal.
