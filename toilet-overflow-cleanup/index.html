Dealing With Toilet Overflow Cleanup in Union City

It is tempting to grab a mop after a toilet overflows, yet the water never stays on top of the tile. It runs under the baseboard, into the wall and through the gap around the drain pipe. Most important is what the water was carrying. Water from the tank or supply line is clean. Water that came up through the bowl is not, and it has to be handled as contaminated.

The first thing our Union City team does is identify the water category, since every later step depends on it. If it is clean, we extract it and dry the area. For gray or black water we wear protective gear, remove soaked porous items such as carpet pad, and disinfect each surface the water reached. Finally we meter the subfloor, the bottoms of the walls and the space underneath, so no moisture gets left behind.

Right Now

What to Do Right Now

  1. Stop the WaterTurn the valve behind the toilet clockwise until it stops. If it will not turn, lift the tank lid and raise the float to stop the fill.
  2. Stop FlushingHold off on flushing, and keep the washer, showers and sinks off too. With a blocked drain line, whatever goes down a drain may return through the toilet.
  3. Stay Out of Contaminated WaterBowl water, or any water with waste in it, means kids and pets stay out of the room. Should you have to cross it, wear gloves and waterproof footwear.
  4. Look at the Ceiling UnderneathIf the toilet is upstairs, go check the ceiling directly below it. A ceiling that sags or drips means turning off that room's power and moving your things clear.
  5. Contain Clean WaterUse old towels to keep clean water from reaching carpet or hallways. Anything contaminated is a job for people with proper gear and disinfecting products.
Why It Happens

Why This Happens

  • A clog in the trap or drain line that sends bowl water over the rim.
  • Roots growing into the sewer line, or a section that has collapsed, slowing all the drains.
  • A full septic tank or saturated drain field that leaves waste water nowhere to go.
  • A cracked tank or failed supply line that floods the bathroom with clean water.
  • Failed wax rings under the toilet, which leak at the floor on each flush.
If It Is Left

What Happens if You Wait

Lingering Odor

Contaminated water that dries inside a wall base or under flooring leaves a smell that returns in humid weather until the source material is taken out.

Subfloor Saturation

Through the grout and around the toilet base, water works its way below tile or vinyl flooring. Once wet, the plywood subfloor swells, its layers separate, and the floor covering bonded on top can come loose.

Bacterial Contamination

Carpet, pad, drywall and baseboards all pick up bacteria from bowl or drain water. Anything porous is normally removed, since it cannot be disinfected with confidence.

Swollen Baseboards and Trim

The bottom edge of a baseboard or door casing draws in water as soon as the floor floods. Since trim is usually fiberboard, it puffs up, cracks apart and stays deformed.

Will Insurance Pay?

Insurance and Toilet Overflow Cleanup

Many homeowners policies cover a toilet overflow when it is sudden and accidental, such as a failed fill valve or an unexpected clog. Unless a backup endorsement was added, policies often exclude water backing up out of a drain or the sewer, which may include an overflow that a blocked main line caused. If the toilet leaked at its base for months, that damage is usually not covered. Your agent or the policy wording can tell you which situation you are in.
