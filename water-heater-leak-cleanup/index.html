Even though the heater itself contains 40 to 80 gallons, the leak is not capped at that amount. Your supply line keeps feeding the tank, which means it fills back up just as quickly as water escapes through a rusted bottom. It will run until the cold water valve gets closed. Where the unit sits decides the damage: a garage floor, a hallway closet or, worst of all, an attic above finished rooms.

Right Now

What to Do Right Now

  1. Cut the Power or GasFor an electric heater, switch off its breaker. With a gas heater, the knob on the control goes to the off position. A burner or heating element must not stay on once the tank is empty.
  2. Close the Cold SupplyThe cold line over the tank has a valve, and it closes with a clockwise turn. A stuck or leaking valve means going to the house main and shutting the water off there.
  3. Beware of Scalding WaterWhat comes out of the tank can cause scalds. Keep children and pets away, and do not wade through pooled water until it has had time to cool.
  4. Drain the Tank SafelyWhen the drain valve is within reach, connect a garden hose and lead it to a floor drain or outdoors, which keeps the rest of the water off the floor.
  5. Clear the FloorGet rugs, boxes and other absorbent things up off the wet floor. Because cardboard and fabric take in water quickly, they hold dampness against the walls and the slab.
Coverage

What About Insurance?

When a tank fails suddenly, the water damage to floors, walls and belongings is often covered as sudden and accidental. Replacing the heater is usually not included, because a tank wearing out from age counts as maintenance. If an adjuster finds signs of a long-term leak, such as rust trails or old staining, the claim can be reduced or denied because gradual leaks usually are not covered. Check your policy, photograph the heater and the damage, and keep the old unit until the claim is settled.

The Usual Culprits

Why This Happens

  • A blocked or rusted drain pan under an attic or closet heater that overflows instead of draining.
  • A drain valve that loosens, cracks or no longer closes all the way.
  • A worn anode rod that no longer protects the tank lining from rust.
  • Fittings left slightly loose by recent repair or installation work.
  • Sediment and corrosion that eat through the bottom of an aging steel tank.
What Is at Risk

What Happens if You Wait

Flooring in Nearby Rooms

Water leaving a heater closet runs under the door and into carpet, laminate or hardwood in the hall. Because the water is hot, wood floors swell faster and the backing on carpet can let go.

Shared Garage Wall

As water runs across the garage slab, it wets the drywall and bottom plate of the wall the garage shares with the house and continues into flooring on the other side.

Items Stored Near the Heater

The first casualties are usually the furniture, boxes and keepsakes stored around the heater in a garage or closet. Wet contents may grow mold unless they are dried or thrown out soon, and cardboard simply collapses.

Heater Closet Drywall

Water wicks up the drywall around an interior heater closet and into the rooms on the other side. The lower wall remains wet on the inside, and the baseboards swell.

What We Do

Our Water Heater Leak Cleanup Process

1

Extract the Water

We remove standing water from the floor, closet or garage and, for attic units, from the overflow pan and ceiling cavity, so the surrounding materials stop absorbing it.

2

Inspect the Surrounding Area

We go over the wall bases, the platform, any ceiling underneath and the adjoining rooms with thermal imaging and moisture meters, which shows how far the wet area extends.

3

Remove Wet Material

Damaged baseboards, drywall that has started to crumble and saturated insulation all get removed. For an attic leak, the wet insulation is cleared out, which lets us dry the drywall and ceiling joists beneath.

4

Dry and Monitor

Drying usually takes 3 to 5 days, and dehumidifiers and air movers run the whole time. Moisture readings are recorded, and you hear from us once the space can take the new heater.
