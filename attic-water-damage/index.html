Attic Water Damage: What Union City Homeowners Should Know

There are two ways an attic ends up wet. Worn out shingles, flashing that has failed, and the seals around chimneys and vents all let rain in from overhead. From below, household moisture rises and condenses on the cold roof sheathing if attic ventilation is poor. Either way, hardly anyone goes up there to look. Our team in Union City inspects the attic, finds the source, and dries it out.

Wet insulation is the heart of most attic jobs. Once wet, both cellulose and fiberglass lose R-value, and heating and cooling the home costs more. The bigger problem is that for weeks, saturated insulation holds moisture on the drywall of the ceiling and on the framing. We remove what is saturated, dry the wood with air movers and dehumidifiers, and treat any mold on the rafters or sheathing.

What It Looks Like

  • An upstairs ceiling where the paint has blistered or started to peel
  • After rain, a stain or brown ring on a top floor ceiling that looks worse
  • Musty odor in upstairs closets or in the attic itself
  • Daylight showing around a vent pipe, chimney, or along a roof valley
  • Dripping, rusted or water marked ducts and metal hardware up in the attic
  • Rafters and roof decking marked with dark streaks or stains

If Nothing Is Done

What Happens if You Wait

First storm

Insulation Soaks It Up

Rain that gets past the roof lands in the insulation, which absorbs it. Nothing shows indoors, so the leak goes unnoticed and the roof is not repaired.

Hours 24 to 48

Mold May Begin

In a still, warm attic, paper facing and wood that stay damp give mold ideal conditions. It can start on the back side of the ceiling drywall and on the sheathing.

Weeks later

Stains Come Through

Once storm after storm has saturated the insulation, the ceiling starts getting wet. Brown rings appear in the room below, and the drywall may start to sag.

Months later

Wood Starts Rotting

The sheathing comes apart in layers and no longer grips the roofing nails. Rafters stay damp enough to decay, and mold covers large areas of the roof deck.

Behind the Surface

What Hides Behind It

Mold on Sheathing

With poor ventilation, mold commonly grows on the bottom of the roof deck. A wide area may be covered by the time somebody climbs up to check.

A Falling Ceiling

Saturated insulation is heavy. The ceiling drywall under it weakens where it is fastened, and it can give way suddenly and bring the wet insulation along.

Insulation That Stops Working

Once damp and packed down, insulation does little of its job and might not recover even when dry. Energy bills climb, and the rooms below the wet area are hotter or colder than the rest.

Bath Fans Venting Inside

Every day, a fan duct that stops short of the roof releases humid bathroom air into the attic. The condensation it leaves on the sheathing can pass for a roof leak.
