Kitchen Water Damage: What Union City Homeowners Should Know

A kitchen has more water connections than any other room: the faucet, the drain, the disposal, the dishwasher and the refrigerator line. Almost every one sits out of view, behind an appliance or inside a cabinet. A drip from any of them travels to the rear of the cabinet base, then spreads beneath the flooring. For homeowners in Union City, our job is to track it down, dry it out, and preserve all of the kitchen that can be saved.

Cabinets are the expensive part of this problem. Plywood and solid wood will often dry successfully, whereas a particleboard box puffs up quickly and never returns to size. Toe kicks come off and a few small, concealed holes are drilled so dry air can travel behind and below the cabinets, which lets cabinets and countertops stay put in many kitchens during drying.

Signs to Look For

  • Swollen or darkened toe kicks, or ones separating from the base of the cabinets
  • Wood or laminate flooring that cups or lifts in front of the dishwasher
  • A water bill that climbs, or a faint hiss or drip sound, with every tap turned off
  • Paint that bubbles, or a stain, on the wall at the back of the sink or dishwasher
  • Doors on the cabinets that have gone out of alignment or started sticking
  • Along the cabinet line or beside the fridge, vinyl that feels soft or looks stained
Behind the Surface

The Damage You Do Not See

Wet Wall Behind Cabinets

Supply and drain lines run through the wall behind the sink and dishwasher. Cabinets fully cover that drywall and framing, so a leak can soak them and mold can spread out of sight.

Refrigerator Water Line

The thin tube feeding the ice maker can leak a few drops a minute. When the fridge never gets moved, this amount will rot the flooring and the wall.

Drips at the Sink Drain

Because it leaks just when the sink drains, a loose fitting or a worn disposal seal often goes unnoticed. Food waste rides along in that water, so the sink cabinet turns smelly and moldy quickly.

Behind the Dishwasher

The subfloor and neighboring cabinet sides get wet when the drain or supply connection drips slowly. The appliance hides all of it until the floor changes.

Why Speed Matters

What Happens if You Wait

The first hour

Water Goes Underneath

A failed fitting or hose sends water under appliances and to the rear of the cabinets. Through edges and seams, it works its way under the floor covering.

Hours 24 to 48

Cabinets Start Swelling

Toe kicks and particleboard cabinet bases absorb the water and swell. Behind the dishwasher and beneath the cabinets, the closed, dark space allows mold to begin.

Day 3 to 7

Floors Begin Moving

Hardwood cups, laminate edges puff up, and vinyl starts to lift as its adhesive lets go. Cabinet doors and drawers drift out of line.

After a week

Removal Becomes Likely

Swollen cabinet boxes cannot be brought back. Mold at the back of the cabinets often forces their removal, countertop included, and the subfloor may be delaminating.

What We Do

How We Handle Kitchen Water Damage

1

Shut Off the Leak

The fixture or appliance line that leaked is shut off, the refrigerator or dishwasher is pulled if necessary, and we confirm the failed connection so it gets repaired before we start drying.

2

Map the Moisture

Using moisture meters and thermal imaging, we check cabinet bases, the wall behind them, and the flooring across the room. Water under a kitchen floor often travels farther than expected.

3

Dry Without Demolition

We remove the toe kicks and direct air behind and beneath the cabinets. Air movers and dehumidifiers run for several days, and we treat damp cavities with an antimicrobial.

4

Take Out Only What Failed

Cabinet sections, flooring or subfloor that cannot be saved are taken out carefully. After dry readings are confirmed, the appliances go back in and we help you arrange the remaining repairs.
