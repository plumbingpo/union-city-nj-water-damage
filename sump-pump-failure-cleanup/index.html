Dealing With Sump Pump Failure Cleanup in Union City

A sump pump has one job, and it tends to fail at the moment it is needed most. Groundwater rises in a big storm and pours into the pit, right as the electricity fails, the float jams or the inflow outruns the pump. Water rises over the rim of the pit and spreads across the basement floor. Until pumping resumes, the water continues to rise.

Do not think of it as clean water. Soil, silt, bacteria and the contents of the drain tile travel with it, so our team rates it no better than Category 2, and Category 3 when it has stood for a while or sewage is involved. Cleanup is different as a result, with wet drywall, carpet pad and other porous materials generally removed and the rest cleaned and disinfected ahead of drying.

Before We Arrive

  1. Keep Out of the BasementDo not step into a flooded basement while the power is on. Water may be touching outlets, the furnace or the pump's own cord.
  2. Shut Off Basement PowerTurn off the basement circuits at the breaker panel, but only if you can reach it from a dry spot. A panel surrounded by water is a job for the power company.
  3. Check the Pump SafelyLook to see whether the pump came unplugged, its outlet tripped or the float is pinned against the pit wall. Reaching into the pit or the water is not safe.
  4. Assume the Water Is DirtyWear rubber boots and gloves if you must go down, keep kids and pets upstairs, and wash your hands after touching anything wet.
  5. Save What Is Dry, Then CallCarry dry valuables upstairs, photograph the water level and the pump, then call us. Waiting for it to recede without help is a mistake.
Coverage

What About Insurance?

Few water claims confuse homeowners as much as this one. A standard homeowners policy usually excludes water that backs up or overflows from a sump, and flood insurance is a separate policy with its own rules. Coverage often exists only if you added a water backup or sump overflow endorsement, and those carry their own limits. Look for that endorsement on your declarations page or ask your agent. To keep the claim file complete, our team documents the damage, the failed pump and the height of the water.

Our Process

What Happens After You Call

1

Get the Water Out

We bring our own pumps and extractors, so nothing depends on your failed sump. Standing water and silt are removed from the floor, the pit area and any low spots.

2

Take Out What Got Wet

Our team wears protective gear to remove wet drywall, insulation, carpet and pad reached by groundwater, then bags and hauls it off, leaving bare framing and concrete.

3

Wash and Disinfect

We wash the framing, the slab and the contents that are staying, then apply an antimicrobial. This happens before drying so contaminants are not blown around the basement.

4

Dry and Verify

Dehumidifiers and air movers run until moisture meter readings on the framing and concrete return to normal, typically 3 to 5 days. Before we pull our equipment, the pit should have a pump that works.

The Usual Culprits

Why This Happens

  • An old motor that burned out after years of cycling on and off.
  • A tripped outlet or breaker, or a plug knocked loose, leaving the pump without power.
  • Stuck or snagged float switches, which never tell the pump to start.
  • A pump too small for the volume of water entering the pit.
  • Water returning down the discharge pipe because the check valve no longer seals.
Why Speed Matters

Where the Damage Spreads

Outlets and Wiring

Rising water can get into receptacles mounted low on basement walls. Any outlet or wiring that got wet should be checked by an electrician before the circuit is turned back on.

Basement Mechanical Equipment

The furnace and water heater frequently sit on the same floor as the pit. Water reaching burners, controls or wiring can ruin them, and each appliance must be inspected before it is used again.

Finished Basement Walls

Drywall and insulation wick dirty water upward from the slab, well above the visible water line. Since it is contaminated water, we remove the wet bottom portion instead of drying it.

Basement Floor Coverings

Groundwater-soaked carpet and padding come out, as cleaning cannot make them safe. Vinyl plank and laminate floors generally have to be pulled as well, since dirty water stays under them.
