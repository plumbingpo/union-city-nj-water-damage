Bathroom Water Damage: What Union City Homeowners Should Know

A bathroom is designed to handle water on its surfaces, never below them. Every flush and every shower lets a small amount of water by after the caulk cracks, the grout wears down or the seal at the toilet fails. That water gathers where you cannot see it, in the subfloor and inside the tiled wall. In Union City, we locate the entry point, measure how far the water has spread, then dry or take out the wet material.

Because the leak is usually slow, the damage tends to be older and deeper than it looks. Under a toilet, the subfloor may be soft from top to bottom even though nothing looks wrong with the tile. Our crew maps the wet area using meters and a thermal camera so nothing extra gets torn out, and only spots that cannot dry where they are get opened.

Warning Signs

  • A brown ring or sagging spot on the ceiling directly below the bathroom
  • Cracked, peeling or mold blackened caulk where the tub or shower meets the wall
  • Soft, springy flooring alongside the tub or where you stand at the vanity
  • Slight rocking when you sit on the toilet, or a dark ring on the floor around it
  • A musty smell that comes back no matter how often the room is cleaned
  • Inside the vanity, a base shelf that is stained, soft or out of shape
The Clock

How the Damage Progresses

The first few days

Water Slips Through

A little water passes the failed grout or caulk every time someone showers. Nothing shows yet, but the subfloor edge and the board behind the tile are absorbing it.

First few weeks

Subfloor Stays Wet

The wood under the tile never gets a chance to dry between showers. Mold starts on the underside of the floor and inside the wall cavity.

One to three months

Signs Start to Appear

Grout darkens, tiles loosen, and the caulk line turns black. If there is a room below, a stain appears on its ceiling and slowly widens.

Six months and beyond

Structure Weakens

Around the tub or toilet the subfloor goes soft, and the joists may start rotting. At this point the job means new tile and carpentry on top of the drying.

Our Process

Our Bathroom Water Damage Process

1

Pinpoint the Leak

Each fixture is tested separately, from the supply lines to the toilet, tub and shower, and thermal imaging and moisture meters show us where water went beneath tile and in the walls.

2

Open What Is Needed

We take out rotted subfloor, soft wall board and tile that has come loose. A toilet leak means contamination, so anything porous that will not come clean is removed.

3

Dry the Hidden Spaces

The subfloor, the framing in the walls and the space above the lower ceiling are dried with air movers and dehumidifiers. Before anything gets closed up, antimicrobial is applied and dry readings are confirmed.

4

Rebuild and Seal

We replace subfloor and backer board, and can coordinate tile, a new toilet seal, fresh caulk and ceiling repair so the leak path is closed, not covered over.

Behind the Surface

The Damage You Do Not See

Rot Under the Toilet

When the wax ring gives out, a bit of water escapes every time the toilet is flushed. It is category 3 water, and the wood surrounding the flange decays until the toilet wobbles.

The Room Below

The stain on the lower ceiling can be several feet off from the leak itself, because water travels along pipes and joists first. Light fixtures and wiring can be in its path.

Leaks Inside the Vanity

Behind the towels and bottles kept under the sink, a drain fitting or supply valve can drip for months. The cabinet floor swells, and the water runs through to the subfloor and the wall behind it.

Mold Behind Tile

Tile and grout are not waterproof on their own. Mold takes hold on the wall board once that hidden layer remains wet, and no scrubbing can get to it.
