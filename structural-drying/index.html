Of all the stages in restoring water damage, drying the structure counts most, and it is the stage most often botched by people without training. Extraction takes care of standing water, but drywall, framing, subflooring, insulation, concrete and other building materials stay loaded with trapped moisture. This trapped moisture must be scientifically removed through controlled evaporation and dehumidification to prevent the mold growth, wood rot, material degradation and odor problems that develop when structures remain wet.

Union City Restoration employs IICRC-certified structural drying technicians who understand psychrometry, the study of temperature, humidity and moisture relationships in air. Using this knowledge, they calculate the specific equipment placement, temperature settings and airflow patterns needed to create the right evaporation conditions for each drying situation. Because hardwood, drywall, plaster and concrete do not dry at the same speed, the method has to change with the material to bring it to acceptable moisture content without harming it further.

Every day of the drying process, we check moisture using thermal imaging cameras, thermo-hygrometers and calibrated moisture meters. Readings are recorded from start to finish in a detailed drying log, which shows moisture dropping step by step in every affected material. Equipment is adjusted daily based on these readings to optimize drying efficiency. We call a job dry when every affected material meets the moisture standard in the IICRC S500 guidelines, and never just because a surface feels dry.

Structural Drying Prevention Tips

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Trust the moisture meter readings, because surfaces can feel dry while moisture remains deep in materials
  • Keep windows and doors closed in areas being dried so the dehumidifiers stay efficient
  • Air movers need a clear path, so avoid stacking things on the equipment or in front of it
  • Daily monitoring only works if someone can let the technician into the property
  • Do not turn off professional drying equipment to reduce noise, because every hour matters
  • Until a technician removes the equipment, keep it running day and night without breaks

What a Professional Brings to Structural Drying

There is real science behind structural drying, which is why guessing does not work. The leading cause of secondary mold after a water event is setting out a few fans and trusting they will be enough. Professional structural drying requires understanding how different materials absorb and release moisture, calculating the grain depression (moisture removal capacity) each situation needs, and monitoring daily to confirm drying is progressing properly. Get it wrong in one direction and mold feeds on leftover moisture, get it wrong in the other and wood or similar materials are damaged by over-drying.

Professional drying equipment is not a bigger version of what stores sell, it is a different kind of machine. Commercial LGR (low-grain refrigerant) dehumidifiers remove 10 to 20 times more moisture from the air than residential dehumidifiers, and high-velocity air movers create the precise airflow patterns needed to accelerate evaporation from structural surfaces. Machines like these cost a great deal and take training to use well, which explains why professional results are so much better than a do-it-yourself effort.

When to Call for Structural Drying in Union City

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Surfaces that stay cool or clammy once the standing water has been removed
  • Gaps opening up in a hardwood floor, or planks with raised edges or centers
  • Humidity that will not come down in the affected space, whatever ventilation you try
  • Stained, swollen or soft spots in the drywall
  • Baseboards or door trim that swell, warp or pull away from the wall
  • Window glass that fogs up near the affected rooms
  • Blistered paint and lifting wallpaper on a wall that got wet
