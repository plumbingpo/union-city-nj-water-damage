Basement Water Prevention Tips in Union City, NJ

Home / Blog / How to Waterproof Your Basement in Union City, NJ

A dry basement protects your home's structure, the air you breathe indoors, and what the property is worth. Many homes in Union City, NJ still deal with basement moisture, anywhere from light seepage to serious flooding. Below, Union City Restoration explains why water gets into basements and which solutions reliably keep a Union City basement dry.

What Makes a Basement Wet

There is more than one route for water to reach a basement, so the first job is to find out which one you have. Soaked soil presses on the foundation, and that hydrostatic pressure drives moisture through any crack, joint, or pore in the concrete. Foundation walls can also crack under the lateral push of expanding clay, which gives water a way inside. Surface water from poor grading, clogged gutters, or weak drainage runs against your foundation and finds a way in. Finally, there is condensation: cool surfaces in a basement pull moisture out of warm humid air, leaving things damp although nothing is leaking.

Waterproofing From the Outside

For lasting results, the best approach is to stop water on the exterior side of the foundation. The work involves digging out around the foundation, coating the walls with a waterproof membrane, and adding or improving the drain tile system outside. It is the most expensive approach, but it addresses the problem at its source and gives the most complete protection. Regrading is also worth the money, because ground that falls 1 inch or more per foot across the first 6 feet away from the foundation carries water off.

Waterproofing From the Inside

Interior waterproofing systems manage water that gets through the foundation instead of keeping it from reaching the foundation. Water that reaches the basement perimeter is caught by an interior French drain, which carries it to the sump pump to be discharged. Sump pump systems, ideally with battery backup, keep water under control during heavy rain and power outages. For significant water intrusion you still need drainage, since coatings and sealants on the inside only offer limited help with efflorescence and small amounts of seepage.

Sump Pump Care and Testing

For Union City homeowners with sump pumps, proper maintenance is essential. Four times a year, pour a bucket of water in the sump pit and watch for the pump to start. Listen for unusual sounds that could point to mechanical problems. Install a battery backup to stay protected during power outages, the most common time for basement flooding. Consider a water-powered backup pump for extra redundancy. Check that discharged water lands a minimum of 6 feet from the house and that a check valve on the line stops backflow.
