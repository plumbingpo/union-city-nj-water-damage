Understanding Professional Structural Drying in Union City, NJ

Home / Blog / How Structural Drying Works in Union City, NJ

Few Union City, NJ homeowners know much about structural drying, even though the rest of a restoration job depends on it. Pumping out the standing water still leaves a great deal of moisture inside the insulation, concrete, subfloor, wood framing, and drywall. If this moisture is not properly removed, it will lead to mold growth, material deterioration, and odor problems. Here we cover how professional drying works and the reasons it outperforms a do-it-yourself effort by a wide margin.

Psychrometry: The Science Behind Drying

Professional structural drying is based on psychrometry, the study of the physical and thermodynamic properties of gas-vapor mixtures. Put simply, it is about the way air movement, relative humidity, and temperature work together to pull water from wet materials. Every job is different, so technicians with IICRC certification calculate the setup psychrometrically, from air mover placement and target humidity and temperature to the number and kind of dehumidifiers.

Drying Equipment Built for the Job

Professional drying gear is in a different class from anything sold for home use. An hour of run time takes 15 to 30 or more pints out of the air with one LGR (Low-Grain Refrigerant) dehumidifier, against 2 to 3 pints with a household model. High-velocity air movers create precisely directed airflow at 2,500 CFM or more to speed evaporation from wet surfaces. Under extreme moisture loads or in cold weather, the specialized tool is a desiccant dehumidifier. All of it costs tens of thousands, and using it well takes specific training.

Each Stage of Professional Drying

Professional structural drying follows a systematic process. It begins with thermal imaging and moisture meters, which are used to map every area the water reached. This map guides equipment placement and establishes baseline moisture readings. The crew then sets air movers and dehumidifiers where the psychrometric math says they will evaporate and capture the most moisture. Adding heat is an option when a quicker evaporation rate is needed. A technician returns daily to take moisture readings at all monitoring points, adjust equipment as needed, and document progress. Drying continues until all affected materials reach acceptable moisture levels compared to dry reference standards.

How Many Days Will Drying Take?

Expect 3 to 5 days for the majority of residential jobs when proper commercial gear is used. Several things set the schedule: the amount and kind of equipment in use, the way the building was built, surrounding conditions, the materials involved, with concrete drying much more slowly than drywall, and the quantity of water present. As a rule, standard drywall needs 2 to 3 days, wood framing 3 to 4 days, and concrete 5 to 7 days or more. After the first moisture readings, the technician gives you an estimated schedule and updates it as the job moves along.

The Reason We Monitor Every Day

Daily monitoring is what separates professional drying from guesswork. Only calibrated readings can tell you if a material is drying at all, if it is on pace, and if it has reached a safe moisture level. Deep in a material, moisture can remain at a dangerous level even though the surface feels dry when touched. Only calibrated moisture meters can provide this verification. Professional drying documentation also gives your insurance company proof that proper industry protocols were followed, supporting your claim and protecting your property value.
