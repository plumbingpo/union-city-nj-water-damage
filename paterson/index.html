What Usually Goes Wrong in Paterson Homes

Water trouble is not all equally urgent, and knowing the difference helps you decide what to do first. The five below are ones we see constantly in Paterson, listed from most pressing to least.

Bursts: Nothing is more time-critical than a broken water line, which keeps pouring until the supply is cut. Depending on the season, the break may follow a freeze, and it often shows only when the ice inside melts. Shut the house supply, run a tap to drain the lines and phone us immediately, whatever the hour. If a ceiling is sagging from the weight of water, stay out from under it and do not poke it.

Runoff: Rain running into the house at ground level ranks a close second. You cannot stop the rain, but you can move belongings up and keep away from any outlets near the water. Having passed over pavement and soil on the way in, it carries dirt and germs. As soon as it is safe to enter, we pump the water out and take away porous items it ruined.

Kitchens: Leaks from a dishwasher, an ice maker line or the plumbing under the kitchen sink should be dealt with the day you find them. Close the small valve under the sink or behind the appliance and the flow usually stops. The harm continues, though, since water has already run under cabinets and flooring where air does not move. In Paterson kitchens we often dry cabinets in place by sending air through small holes in the toe kick.

Crawlspaces: Moisture under a raised floor is seldom an emergency, but it should not be put off for a season. When we look underneath, we tend to find water lying on the liner, insulation that is soaked or sweating ducts. Given time, that humidity works its way upstairs and the joists start to rot. Our crew clears any standing water, brings the framing down to a normal moisture level and reports the likely cause.

Odors: A musty smell near the site of an old leak is the least urgent item here, yet it deserves an inspection soon. Since growth can start 24 to 48 hours after materials get wet, an old leak that was never fully dried is a likely culprit.
